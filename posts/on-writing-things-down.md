I've kept a notebook since I was fourteen. Not a journal, exactly — more like a place where I put things that feel too slippery to hold onto otherwise.

## Why Writing Works

There's something that happens when you try to put a thought into sentences. The thought resists. It turns out to have more edges than you thought. You discover you only partially believed it.

This is the useful thing: writing is a form of *thinking*, not just a record of thoughts that already happened. The sentence that comes out of the pen is not the sentence you expected to write, and that gap between them is where the work is.

## On Notebooks Specifically

Notebooks have a texture that screens don't. You can draw a margin note that spirals in on itself. You can underline a word four times and each underline can have a different emotional weight. You can close the notebook and feel the contents physically present.

I don't romanticize this. I type most of my serious work. But I think there's a reason the notebook survives.
